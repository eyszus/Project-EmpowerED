Project EmpowerED

Every reading gap today can become an opportunity gap tomorrow

Reading proficiency outcomes among Filipino students are analyzed by this project, and how socioeconomic and geographic factors contribute to differences in literacy performance across public schools is investigated.

Overview

Project EmpowerED investigates literacy outcomes in the Philippines using data-driven analysis, focusing on socioeconomic status, parental education, and geographic location as drivers of educational inequality.

Problem

The Philippines continues to experience severe literacy challenges, reflected in poor reading performance and significant educational disparities affecting low-income and rural students.

Solution

Use statistical and data-driven methods to identify patterns behind literacy inequality and provide evidence-based insights.

Background

Literacy gaps affect future opportunities. Educational inequality is tied to structural barriers, and socioeconomic and geographic factors play major roles in who succeeds in reading and who is left behind.

Recent international education assessments have placed the Philippines among the lowest performing countries in reading comprehension. Students from low-income households or rural communities may face limited access to learning resources, fewer educational opportunities, and structural disadvantages that affect literacy development.

Filipino students literacy crisis — EdCom 2 report

National review highlights deepening reading gaps and urgent calls to address the literacy crisis among Filipino learners.

New study: 7 in 10 students in Philippines lack basic reading skills

Research underscores how widespread foundational reading deficits remain across the Philippine education system.

Research Questions

  • How does the reading proficiency of Filipino students compare to that of other Southeast Asian students in the PISA dataset?
  • To what degree do household income and parental education correlate with standardized reading comprehension scores in the Philippines?
  • Is there a statistically significant difference in literacy outcomes between students from urban and rural public elementary schools?

Hypotheses

  • Filipino students will have lower reading proficiency compared to the latest Southeast Asian benchmarks from PISA.
  • Lower household income and parental education will be associated with lower reading comprehension scores.
  • Students from rural public schools will demonstrate significantly lower literacy outcomes than students from urban schools.

Data

Multiple educational datasets are analyzed. Literacy outcomes are compared across demographic and geographic variables, and statistical methods are used to identify relationships and disparities.

Detailed statistical interpretation

Hypothesis 3: Urban–Rural Disparity

Hypothesis: Students from rural public schools show significantly lower literacy outcomes.

A Pearson correlation analysis was conducted to determine whether a statistically significant relationship exists between urbanity level and literacy rate across regions (Hypothesis 3). The results produced a correlation coefficient of r = 0.504, indicating a moderate positive relationship between the two variables. This suggests that regions with higher levels of urbanization tend to have higher literacy rates.

The p-value obtained from the test was p = 0.0328, which is less than the commonly accepted significance level of α = 0.05. Therefore, the null hypothesis stating that there is no significant relationship between urbanity level and literacy rate is rejected.

Further analysis using linear regression produced a slope of 0.0712 and an intercept of 85.7517. The positive slope indicates that for every one percentage point increase in urbanity level, the literacy rate is expected to increase by approximately 0.071 percentage points.

The coefficient of determination, R² = 0.254, indicates that approximately 25.44% of the variation in literacy rate can be explained by differences in urbanity level. While this demonstrates that urbanity contributes to literacy outcomes, it also suggests that other factors—such as education access, socioeconomic conditions, infrastructure, and government programs—likely play a substantial role in influencing literacy rates.

Overall, the statistical analysis confirms that there is a statistically significant, moderate positive relationship between urbanity level and literacy rate among regions. However, the relatively modest R² value indicates that urbanity level alone does not fully explain variations in literacy, and additional variables would be needed for a more comprehensive model.
