Project EmpowerED

A data-driven look at literacy outcomes across the Philippines.

This project investigates the state of reading proficiency among Filipino students and the factors that may influence literacy outcomes in public schools. Literacy is a key foundation for quality education and long-term national development, yet international assessments have consistently shown that Filipino students struggle in reading comprehension compared to many other countries.

Through a data-driven analysis, our project examines how socioeconomic status and geographic location relate to reading proficiency outcomes. By analyzing datasets from international and national education studies, we aim to identify patterns that can help explain disparities in literacy performance across the Philippines.

Photo: Philstar

The Problem

Recent international education assessments, particularly the Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA), have placed the Philippines among the lowest performing countries in reading comprehension. This raises important questions about the underlying factors that shape literacy outcomes among Filipino students.

Educational inequality may stem from differences in socioeconomic status, parental education, and geographic location. Students from low-income households or rural communities may face limited access to learning resources, fewer educational opportunities, and structural disadvantages that affect literacy development.

Research Questions

  • How does the reading proficiency of Filipino students compare to that of other Southeast Asian students in the PISA dataset?
  • To what degree do household income and parental education correlate with standardized reading comprehension scores in the Philippines?
  • Is there a statistically significant difference in literacy outcomes between students from urban and rural public elementary schools?

Hypotheses

  • Filipino students will have lower reading proficiency compared to the latest Southeast Asian benchmarks from PISA.
  • Lower household income and parental education will be associated with lower reading comprehension scores.
  • Students from rural public schools will demonstrate significantly lower literacy outcomes than students from urban schools.

Approach

To investigate these questions, we analyze multiple datasets related to education and socioeconomic indicators. These include international large-scale assessments and national statistics datasets that provide information about literacy performance, household income, parental education, and geographic classification of schools.

Statistical analysis will be used to measure correlations between socioeconomic indicators and reading scores, while comparative analysis will evaluate differences between urban and rural student populations.

Data Sources

Exploratory Data Analysis

Our analysis examines literacy patterns across the Philippines through three complementary research questions. Below are the key visualizations from our exploratory data analysis.

RQ1: Regional Comparison

How does the reading proficiency of Filipino students compare to other Southeast Asian students?

We compared reading proficiency scores of Filipino students against other Southeast Asian nations using PISA 2022 data. The visualizations reveal overall trends in regional literacy performance and highlight areas where the Philippines differs from neighboring countries in student achievement levels.

RQ2: Socioeconomic Factors

To what degree do household income and parental education correlate with reading scores?

Socioeconomic status—measured by household income and parental education—significantly influences student literacy outcomes. Our analysis explores the relationship between these factors and reading proficiency, revealing how economic disparities translate into educational achievement gaps. Multiple visualizations examine correlation patterns and distribution differences across income and education levels.

RQ3: Urban vs. Rural Differences

Is there a statistically significant difference in literacy outcomes between urban and rural schools?

Geographic location fundamentally shapes educational access and outcomes. This analysis compares reading proficiency between urban and rural public elementary schools in the Philippines. The visualizations demonstrate disparities in literacy performance and highlight the structural challenges facing rural education systems.

Hypothesis Testing Results

We conducted statistical tests to evaluate our primary hypotheses. The results provide evidence about literacy disparities in the Philippines:

Hypothesis 3: Urban-Rural Disparity

Hypothesis: Students from rural public schools show significantly lower literacy outcomes.

Hypothesis Testing: Pearson Correlation Analysis

A Pearson correlation analysis was conducted to determine whether a statistically significant relationship exists between urbanity level and literacy rate across regions (Hypothesis 3). The results produced a correlation coefficient of r = 0.504, indicating a moderate positive relationship between the two variables. This suggests that regions with higher levels of urbanization tend to have higher literacy rates.

The p-value obtained from the test was p = 0.0328, which is less than the commonly accepted significance level of α = 0.05. Therefore, the null hypothesis stating that there is no significant relationship between urbanity level and literacy rate is rejected. This indicates that the observed relationship is statistically significant and unlikely to have occurred by random chance.

Further analysis using linear regression produced a slope of 0.0712 and an intercept of 85.7517. The positive slope indicates that for every one percentage point increase in urbanity level, the literacy rate is expected to increase by approximately 0.071 percentage points. This supports the earlier observation from the scatterplot that the relationship between the variables is positive.

The coefficient of determination, R² = 0.254, indicates that approximately 25.44% of the variation in literacy rate can be explained by differences in urbanity level. While this demonstrates that urbanity contributes to literacy outcomes, it also suggests that other factors, such as education access, socioeconomic conditions, infrastructure, and government programs, likely play a substantial role in influencing literacy rates.

Overall, the statistical analysis confirms that there is a statistically significant, moderate positive relationship between urbanity level and literacy rate among regions. However, the relatively modest R² value indicates that urbanity level alone does not fully explain variations in literacy, and additional variables would be needed for a more comprehensive model.

Key Takeaways

  • Filipino students consistently underperform compared to regional benchmarks
  • Socioeconomic status is a strong predictor of reading achievement
  • Geographic location creates significant educational inequities
  • Structural barriers in rural areas compound literacy challenges
